## Travel Question

The chapter answers one question: which single hillside visit adds the most to
this Lanzhou day?

The three choices are not ranked versions of the same panorama:

`central crossing -> White Pagoda Hill`

`broad river-valley geometry -> Lanshan and Santai Pavilion`

`park paths and a layered historic building group -> Wuquan Mountain`

The traveler chooses after checking visibility, precipitation, wind,
daylight, path condition, mobility, and the return route. A poor view is not a
reason to climb higher. None of the three is assumed to be step-free, and no
cableway, vehicle, bus, gate, or through-path is treated as permanently
available.

## Rejected Claims

- `White Pagoda Hill, Lanshan, and Wuquan Mountain can be completed as one
  efficient day.` Rejected: they answer different questions and require
  separate current checks and returns.
- `Lanshan always provides a clear panorama or sunset.` Rejected: visibility,
  weather, daylight, and access are variable.
- `Route 750, a cableway, a minibus, or a through-path is always operating.`
  Rejected: the found evidence is dated and in one case explicitly holiday
  only.
- `Wuquan is easy or accessible because it is a city park.` Rejected: it is a
  hillside site with routes and levels that need direct confirmation.
- `The five springs were created when Huo Qubing struck the ground.` Rejected
  as a popular legend, not established history.
- `Every Wuquan building is Yuan-period, every White Pagoda Hill roof is
  ancient, or the current Santai Pavilion is the Ming original.` Rejected:
  each landscape contains documented later construction and repair.
- Exact walking times, fares, opening hours, gate names, cableway boarding
  points, last descents, and guaranteed night access are excluded until a live
  operator or site record supports the travel date.
