# Lanzhou River Valley Orientation Plate

Create a high-resolution, text-free, realistic editorial travel image for a B6
pocket guide. Landscape `16:9`, 4K.

## Location And Composition

- View from a public overlook on the White Pagoda Hill side of central
  Lanzhou, looking across the Yellow River toward the south-bank city. Angle
  the camera slightly down and along the river so the steel-truss Zhongshan
  Bridge is clearly visible crossing the river near the center of the image.
- The Yellow River, the elongated urban valley, close dry mountain ridges, and
  Zhongshan Bridge must be recognizable. The river and city occupy most of the
  frame; the bridge is visible but this is a broad orientation view, not a
  bridge close-up.
- Match the bridge structure in the location reference: five low, repeated
  steel through-truss spans on river piers, with straight truss portals. Do not
  turn it into a suspension bridge, cable-stayed bridge, arch bridge, or a
  single invented span. Do not add towers, suspension cables, or modern pylons.
- Use clear late-afternoon daylight with realistic north-west China color and
  moderate atmospheric distance. Do not add a fantasy sunset, spotless alpine
  air, impossible turquoise water, dramatic cliffs, or invented monuments.
- Keep the foreground overlook simple and plausible. No raster text, logos,
  trademarks, lettering, signs, captions, map graphics, or symbols on any
  character, clothing, notebook, or robot panel.

## Four Guide Continuity

Include exactly four travelers, all clearly distinct and based on the supplied
references: Aya-chan, Lala Xia, Sasa-kun, and the Zhuangzi robot. The robot is
an equal friend and traveler, not luggage or equipment. Preserve each guide's
recognizable face, hair, clothing language, proportions, and color cues.

Stage the four friends together at the lower-right edge of the overlook in a
three-quarter front view. Every face and both eyes must be visible to the
reader while the group gestures naturally toward the river behind them. Aya is
the red-panda schoolgirl in a navy sailor uniform; Lala Xia is the round panda
in cream overalls; Sasa-kun is the human boy with brown hair and a panda hood,
not a second animal; the Zhuangzi robot is the white-and-black humanoid robot.
They should occupy no more than the lower quarter of the frame. The destination
remains dominant. Do not show the group from behind. Do not add any fifth
person, distant anonymous tourist, duplicate guide, cropped face, animal
substitute, or historical eyewitness scene.

The patchwork notebook may appear closed in Aya-chan's hand if it remains
secondary. Do not force the LightMind glasses or word-card device into this
scene.

## Visual Character

Realistic editorial travel photography with tactile, carefully constructed
miniature guide figures integrated into a real city overlook. Natural skin,
fabric, metal, stone, vegetation, river, and urban detail. Vivid but credible
white, vermilion, jade, cobalt, and coral accents; no dull yellow cast, no
purple gradient, no decorative bokeh, and no poster typography.

## Factual Limits

The two location references establish the river-valley shape, central bridge,
south-bank skyline, dry ridges, and overlook relationship. They are visual
evidence only and are not copied. Do not promise a particular river color,
boat service, crowd level, skyline clarity, season, or exact fixed viewpoint.
