## Travel Question

The chapter answers one question: how should a traveler cross Zhongshan Bridge,
read what changed at the crossing, and choose a realistic White Pagoda Hill
visit without turning the riverfront into an attraction checklist?

The route is fixed:

`south-bank approach -> Zhongshan Bridge -> north-bank park entrance -> lower
terraces or full climb -> one optional riverfront segment or return`

White Pagoda Hill is the destination reached by the crossing, not a second
unrelated outing. Waterwheel Park, boats, another hill, and a long promenade are
not compulsory additions. Chapter 7 will compare White Pagoda Hill with the
other city heights; Chapter 11 retains Ink Danxia, Liujiaxia/Bingling Temple,
and the conditional Yellow River-Tao River confluence branch.

   and White Pagoda Hill before moving.
2. **Map:** cross once, then choose lower terraces or the full climb; keep one
   optional riverfront exit.
3. **Practical:** make a live crowd, weather, river, and access check; the
   bridge is pedestrian, but temporary controls remain possible.
4. **History at an object:** use the General Pillar to explain the floating
   bridge and the problem of seasonal dismantling and rebuilding.
5. **Read the steel:** distinguish the `1909` fixed bridge from the curved
   profile added in the `1954` reinforcement.
6. **Figure:** show the present White Pagoda and temple precinct as the
   destination, with all four guides small and secondary.
7. **Practical:** choose the lower-terrace visit or the full climb according to
   legs, weather, visibility, daylight, and current path access.
8. **Cultural context:** read the `1958-1960` park composition together with
   the documented pre-`1448`, Ming, and Qing temple layers, without inventing
   one continuous origin story.
9. **Callout:** from the viewpoint or lower terrace, choose one riverfront
   segment or return; protect the next meal and route instead of adding stops.
