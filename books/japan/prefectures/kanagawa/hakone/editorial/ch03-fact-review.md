## Chapter Question

The chapter answers one question: **where is the mountain railway itself worth
attention, and when is an intermediate stop worth the time?** It follows the
ride from Yumoto through the switchbacks, gives the Open-Air Museum one honest
time block, and ends at Gora's cable-car decision. It does not preview the
Owakudani crossing handled by Chapter 4.

## Rejected Shortcuts

- "Sit on one fixed side for the best view." Rejected. Switchbacks reverse the
  train's direction and scenery changes; no side wins for the full climb.
- "Get off at every interesting station." Rejected. The line is short but each
  stop consumes a real travel block. Choose the lodging stop or one principal
  attraction.
- "Hydrangeas line the railway." Rejected as year-round prose. Bloom and any
  special service are seasonal and require a current dated source.
- "The whole railway has an `80‰` grade." Rejected. It is the published maximum.
- A reproduced museum sculpture or branded pavilion in generated art.
  Rejected. The location figure may show terrain, paths, scale, and generic
  distant forms, but no protected artwork is copied.
- "The museum takes exactly two hours." Rejected. Use the museum's ranges and
  let weather, mobility, interest, and open exhibitions alter the visit.

## Block Spine

1. Watch the four guides read the reversal at Ohiradai instead of treating it as delay.
2. Read the slope map from published elevations and switchback points.
3. Connect the 1919 engineering decisions to what the train is doing now.
4. Ride for the changing direction and valley scale without chasing one seat.
5. Leave the train at Chokoku-no-Mori and let the museum landscape open out.
6. Give the museum a real `90-120` minute block and use indoor space as weather cover.
7. Choose a short cafe pause, a full meal, or brought food, and use only the permitted eating areas.
8. At Gora, choose between continuing uphill and protecting the lodging plan.
9. Hand the cable-car climb to the Owakudani chapter.
