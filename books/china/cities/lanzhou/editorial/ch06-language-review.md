# Chapter 6 Language Review

Status: Chinese, Japanese, and English accepted on `2026-08-22` after
independent line edits and compiled B6/mobile review.

## Shared Route

All ten blocks serve one food day: breakfast at a beef-noodle shop, one clear
counter order, a midday return to the main route, one afternoon snack branch,
an optional seated lily-and-tea pause, and a firm evening stop. Food history
appears only where it helps the traveler recognise a craft, ingredient, or menu
term. The chapter does not become a restaurant list or an origin story.

## Chinese

The Chinese pass uses direct counter and table language: `报面型`, `先看分量`,
`问能否分放`, and `错过一站，不必折返`. Noodle width is treated as a texture
choice, not a test of expertise. The final pass removed abstract claims about
authenticity and rewrote the day clock as a practical way to avoid overeating,
food waste, and cross-city detours.

## Japanese

The Japanese pass was edited as its own guide. It uses ordinary service terms
such as `会計`, `食券`, `受け取り口`, `別添え`, and `営業中`, while retaining
recognisable Chinese menu names. The final pass removed literal Chinese syntax
and ambiguous compounds, then checked every rendered furigana line at B6.

## English

The English pass leads with executable decisions: choose one morning bowl,
show one noodle-shape term, ask about the actual serving, and return to the
route. It distinguishes protected craft from founder stories, GI status from a
menu label, and fermentation from a universal alcohol claim. Phrases that
sounded like slogans or moral summaries were replaced with concrete eating and
movement advice.

## Alignment Result

The three languages align by meal, object, question, and stop rule rather than
sentence shape. Each keeps volatile hours, prices, recipes, shop operations,
and dietary status behind a same-day check. Each also makes one snack a complete
choice and lets the reader finish the day without collecting every name.
