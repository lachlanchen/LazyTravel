# Chapter 1 Fact Ledger: Read the Ground Before the Timeline

Ledger date: `2026-08-14`. Destination gate: `china/cities/xian`.

This ledger controls what Chapter 1 may say. It is not draft prose. Claims are
kept narrower than the source material, and locators point to the evidence that
was actually checked. The 1981 book supplies historical leads; modern official
and institutional sources decide whether those leads remain usable.

## Rejected or Deferred Claims

| Claim | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| "Xi'an was the capital of exactly thirteen dynasties." | Deferred. Counting rules differ, and the number does not help Chapter 1 explain space. |
| "Guanzhong means the land inside Hangu, Wuguan, Dasan, and Xiaoguan." | Rejected as an unqualified definition. It is one later explanation among several. |
| "Eight rivers still encircle the old city." | Rejected. The phrase is historical and literary; the waterways do not form a literal ring around the Ming wall. |
| "The current street grid is the Tang grid." | Deferred to Chapter 2. Continuities exist, but modern roads, the Ming core, and the Sui-Tang plan must be distinguished precisely. |
| Exact climate normals, best seasons, river flow, water quality, closures, and trail access. | Deferred to dated practical blocks in Chapter 11 or relevant attraction chapters. |
| Descriptions of the landscape as pristine, timeless, or untouched. | Rejected. They erase modern engineering, urbanization, ecological repair, and seasonal variation. |

## Language Pass Briefs

- **Chinese:** local precision first; retain `塬` for the landform and the
  standard Chinese river names. Avoid textbook cadence and four-character
  slogan chains.
- **Japanese:** write as independent travel prose. Explain `関中` and `塬`
  through spatial context rather than a parenthetical dictionary gloss.
- **English:** prefer directional and relational language. Keep pinyin only
  where it helps identify a map label; do not flatten Chang'an into "old
  Xi'an."
