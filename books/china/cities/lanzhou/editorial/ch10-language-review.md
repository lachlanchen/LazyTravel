# Chapter 10 Language Review

Status: Chinese, Japanese, and English editorial text accepted on `2026-08-23`,
including the compiled B6 pages and `390 px` responsive reading review.

## Shared Route

All ten blocks answer one departure question: where will the traveler sleep
next, and which station or terminal is printed on the valid booking? The
chapter begins with those two decisions, then works backward through bags,
checkout, the city transfer, wayfinding inside the departure building, checks,
and a margin. Lanzhou West, Lanzhou Station, and Zhongchuan T3 remain three
exclusive gates. Direction, train class, and an old guide never substitute for
the live booking.

The Hexi Corridor appears only to explain why Wuwei, Zhangye, Jiayuguan, and
Dunhuang must be treated as separate overnight choices. It is not turned into
a one-day attraction list. One final Lanzhou stop survives only when it lies
between the bags and the booked gate; otherwise the stop is removed.

## Chinese

The Chinese pass uses direct planning language: `下一夜住宿地`, `订单上的出发点`,
`从右往左倒推`, `行李与退房`, and `最后一处整项删掉`. It distinguishes
`兰州西站`, `兰州站`, and `中川机场T3` throughout. `高铁`, `向西`, or the
word `机场` is never presented as enough information to choose one of them.

The final-stop paragraph was rewritten around a physically possible sequence:
collect bags, leave the hotel, keep at most one same-segment stop, and continue
to the departure point without returning for luggage. The priority-assistance
paragraph dates its six-hour and 60-minute checks and immediately tells the
reader that the timings can change.

## Japanese

The Japanese pass was edited independently around ordinary booking and
departure terms: `次の宿泊地`, `予約に記載された出発地`, `券面`, `荷物を受け取る`,
`市内横断`, and `余裕`. It uses `蘭州西駅`, `蘭州駅`, and `中川空港T3`
consistently, while `中川空港東駅` is limited to the current rail-stop name.

Literal or bureaucratic phrasing was removed where it obscured movement. The
reader now chooses one overnight city, verifies one booked gate, and cuts one
optional stop. Historical context about the Hexi Corridor remains attached to
that choice rather than becoming a detached regional summary.

## English

The English pass replaces shorthand such as “printed gate” with `departure
point shown on the booking`, and keeps `printed station` only where the reader
must copy the station name. It says `next overnight city` rather than treating
western Gansu as one continuous destination. The Lanzhou Station caption now
correctly calls the trip from western lodging an `eastbound cross-city
transfer`.

Operational prose distinguishes recognition from navigation: a roof, clock,
or terminal hall may help identify a building but cannot reveal the entrance,
ticket gate, waiting hall, security point, or walking time. No current train,
flight, fare, or connection is promised.

## Alignment Result

The three languages align by decision and consequence rather than sentence
shape. Durable advice is separated from service patterns, terminal assignment,
assistance lead times, and hotel transfers that require a dated recheck. The
prose is closed unless a concrete factual, reading, layout, or parity failure is
demonstrated.
