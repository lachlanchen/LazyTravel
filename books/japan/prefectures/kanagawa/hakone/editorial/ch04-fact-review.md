## Chapter Question

The chapter answers one question: **how much time should a traveler give
Owakudani while crossing from Sounzan to Togendai?** The route, public
overlook, black eggs, seated meal, and reserved nature trail are distinct
choices. Volcanic history explains what is visible; it does not interrupt the
travel decision or turn the crossing into a general geology essay.

## Rejected Shortcuts

- "Ride the ropeway for Mount Fuji." Rejected as the chapter line. The active
  volcanic landscape remains worth reading when Fuji is hidden.
- "White steam looks safe today." Rejected. Visitors cannot infer gas safety
  from appearance or smell.
- "A ropeway ticket includes the nature trail." Rejected. Public access,
  transport, and trail entry are separate.
- "Eating one egg adds seven years of life." Retained only as local folklore,
  never as health advice.
- "Black eggs are lunch" or "curry is traditional Hakone food." Rejected.
  The chapter distinguishes snack, quick food, and seated meal.
- "Wait for the next service to restart." Rejected as a plan. Follow staff and
  reroute from the last confirmed-open transport point.
- Generated figures as exact documentary camera records. Rejected. They show
  a factual place class, visible barriers, route logic, and traveler scale.

## Block Spine

1. Leave Sounzan and read vents, vegetation edges, and maintenance roads before looking for Fuji.
2. Choose the public stop, meal stop, or booked trail before boarding uphill.
3. Read the public overlook as a managed volcanic place with a specific history.
4. Check ropeway, overlook, and trail safety separately.
5. Eat a black egg while keeping chemistry, folklore, and current sales distinct.
6. Decide whether the stop needs a snack or a seated lunch.
7. Treat the nature trail as reserved, controlled entry rather than an extension of the overlook.
8. Protect the booking, lodging dinner, and return connection when service changes.
9. Descend to Togendai and hand the lake decision to Chapter 5.
