# Chapter 4 Fact Ledger: Let Text Lead

Ledger date: `2026-08-15`. Destination gate: `china/cities/xian`.

This ledger controls the claims used in Chapter 4. *Xi'an Shihua* provides a
useful historical sequence for the pagodas and the formation of Beilin; the
family guide shows the familiar visitor route and the folklore that now needs
checking. Neither source controls current access, conservation practice, or
modern interpretation. Current museum records, heritage documentation, and
scholarship are used to verify those leads. The Chinese and English open guides
remain read-only orientation sources; their hours, prices, rankings, and prose
are not reused.

## Rejected Or Deferred Claims

| Claim | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| "If you have not visited the Big Wild Goose Pagoda, you have not visited Xi'an." | Rejected as promotional gatekeeping. The chapter compares what each site lets the reader understand. |
| The pagoda-name goose legend as historical explanation. | Rejected from factual prose. At most it belongs in a clearly labelled folklore note, which this compact chapter does not need. |
| The Small Wild Goose Pagoda's earthquake crack closed because its foundation was deliberately built as a hemisphere. | Rejected as unsupported pseudo-engineering repeated by the family guide. |
| The tower has survived more than seventy earthquakes and is therefore exceptionally earthquake-proof. | Rejected. Event counts and structural conclusions are poorly sourced, and past survival is not a safety guarantee. |
| "Beilin is the world's heaviest book," "a sea of calligraphy," or another collection superlative. | Rejected. These phrases replace inspection with advertising and obscure how the stones were arranged and used. |
| A visitor should make a rubbing directly from a museum stele. | Rejected. Any educational demonstration must use an authorized modern replica or non-contact method. |
| `Nestorian` as the unqualified name of the 781 stele or its community. | Rejected. Use `Jingjiao` and Church of the East; retain the older name only as a search alias with a terminology note. |
| Tang Chang'an was wholly tolerant because several religions left inscriptions there. | Rejected as an inference too broad for the evidence. The chapter may establish documented presence, patronage, translation, and later suppression or loss where sources permit. |
| The 2013 guide's tickets, opening hours, climb access, souvenir advice, and museum layout. | Rejected as stale. Current operations come from dated official notices and remain explicitly volatile. |
| A fast route through all three sites in one morning. | Rejected. It converts text-rich museums into exterior photo stops and ignores separate security, booking, and transfer time. |
| A full treatment of Shaanxi History Museum. | Deferred. It is not needed to sustain this chapter's written-word route and would overload the B6 chapter. Relevant museum choices return in the itineraries. |

## Language Pass Briefs

- **Chinese:** distinguish `大慈恩寺`, `大雁塔`, `荐福寺`, `小雁塔`,
  `西安博物院`, `碑林`, and `西安碑林博物馆`. Prefer verbs such as `校勘`,
  `誊写`, `刻石`, `迁置`, and `传拓`; avoid strings of four-character praise.
- **Japanese:** use `玄奘`, `義浄`, `大慈恩寺`, `薦福寺`, `碑林`, and
  `景教碑` consistently. Explain institutional translation in natural Japanese
  rather than mapping Chinese syntax. Mark disputed terminology and inference
  with ordinary, unobtrusive qualifications.
- **English:** use "translation team," "inscription," "rubbing," and "Church
  of the East" precisely. Avoid "timeless," "treasure house," "East meets
  West," and the false opposition between sacred history and practical travel.

## Visual Evidence Rules

- `asset-xian-written-word-route-map` is an original schematic at one common
  scale. It locates the Big Wild Goose Pagoda, Small Wild Goose Pagoda/Xi'an
  Museum, and Beilin against the generalized Tang outer city and the present
  city wall. It is explicitly an orientation graphic, not a street-navigation
  map or a claim that the modern route follows Tang roads.
- `asset-xian-rubbing-replica-demonstration` is a high-resolution realistic
  generated editorial image. It shows hands making a traditional ink rubbing
  from a plainly modern replica slab in a controlled teaching workspace. No
  legible historical inscription, real artefact, real museum, or identifiable
  person is depicted.
- The figure follows the same restrained, natural-light editorial treatment as
  the approved Chapter 3 conservation image. Real material texture and usable
  hand positions matter more than cinematic atmosphere. No costume scene,
  sepia filter, decorative smoke, dramatic spotlight, text, or watermark.
- The prompt, generator, dimensions, checksum, captions, rights, and visual QA
  are recorded. All three captions state `AI-generated`, `non-documentary`, and
  `modern replica`; the prose states that touching or rubbing originals is not
  permitted merely because the process is illustrated.
- Map, figure, and prose remain separate forms of evidence. The map carries no
  volatile timetable; the generated image supports material understanding but
  no historical claim.
