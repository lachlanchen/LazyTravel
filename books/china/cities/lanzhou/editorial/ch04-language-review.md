# Chapter 4 Language Review

Status: Chinese, Japanese, and English accepted on `2026-08-21` after
independent line edits and compiled B6/mobile review.

## Shared Route

All nine blocks follow one outing: approach Zhongshan Bridge from the old-city
side, read the former floating crossing and the 1909/1954 steel layers, enter
White Pagoda Hill Park, choose the lower terraces or White Pagoda Temple, then
add at most one riverfront segment. The history explains objects visible on
that route and does not turn other riverfront attractions into a checklist.

## Chinese

The Chinese pass uses ordinary movement and decision language such as `避开人流`,
`原路折返`, `到北端入口再决定`, and `只沿河选一个方向`. It replaces production
phrases about a “next page” with the visible distinction between the modern
park terraces and older temple precinct. The lower terraces remain a complete
visit rather than a failed climb.

## Japanese

The Japanese pass was rewritten as a guide rather than a literal translation.
It uses `橋詰で確認する`, `上り道がどこまで開いているか`, and
`北岸側へ抜ける`, while removing translated expressions about a “main line,”
“proving the highest point,” and moving the hill to a “time segment.” The
floating bridge, fixed crossing, temple precinct, and modern park layers remain
distinct.

## English

The English pass keeps one practical subject at a time: recognize the
bridge-hill-pagoda alignment, check current access, compare floating and fixed
crossings, choose a climb depth, and protect the return. Production wording
such as “next block,” “departure clock,” and “collect a waterwheel site” was
removed. The final callout was tightened once to clear the B6 page without
discarding a travel decision.

## Alignment Result

The three languages align by place, evidence, and traveler choice rather than
sentence count. None presents the entire bridge as unchanged since 1909,
assigns every roof to the Yuan period, promises a step-free route, freezes a
walking time, or makes boats and other hills compulsory. Reader-facing captions
describe the route; generation and visual-reference details remain in
provenance.
