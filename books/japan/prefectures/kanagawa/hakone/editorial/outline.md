# Hakone Editorial Outline

Title: **Hakone Pocket Travel Guide**.

Hakone is not a compact town that can be understood from a list of sights. It
is a mountain journey made from connected pieces: gateway railways, a steep
local line, cable car, ropeway, buses, lake crossings, old roads, and places to
sleep between them. The book's main line is therefore simple: **read the
height, choose one coherent crossing, and let each stop explain the landscape
and history that made it useful**.

This is a travel guide, not a catalogue and not a general history. Geology
explains the gradients, fumaroles, lake, weather, and hot springs. Tokaido
history explains the road, checkpoint, tea house, and the strain of crossing
the pass. Modern tourism history explains why railways, museums, ryokan, and
resort districts sit where they do. Every historical passage must change what
the traveler notices or how the day is planned.

The book has exactly 11 chapters. The count is locked because each chapter has
a distinct travel decision; merging any two would overload a pocket chapter,
while splitting them would create thin filler.

Milestone status on `2026-08-21`: all 11 chapters are reviewed and
reproducibly built as one complete B6 pocket book and synchronized website.

## Chapter Spine

1. **先读山：火山口、湖与高差**<br>
   **まず山を読む：カルデラ、湖、標高差**<br>
   **Read the Mountain First: Caldera, Lake, and Elevation**

   Establish the outer ridges, central cones, Lake Ashi, Owakudani, valleys,
   and the large change in height between Odawara, Yumoto, Gora, and the lake.
   The first map teaches why weather and transfer time can differ sharply
   within one small-looking destination. Mount Fuji is a possible view, never
   a promised backdrop.

2. **小田原到汤本：把换乘放进行程**<br>
   **小田原から湯本へ：乗り換えを旅程に入れる**<br>
   **Odawara to Yumoto: Make Transfers Part of the Day**

   Choose the gateway, ticket strategy, loop direction, and luggage plan.
   Explain the difference between arriving from Tokyo and continuing between
   eastern and western Japan. Treat passes, timetables, fares, service status,
   and baggage cutoffs as dated information rather than permanent facts.

3. **登山电车往强罗：坡度、艺术与停留**<br>
   **登山電車で強羅へ：勾配、アート、立ち止まる時間**<br>
   **Climb to Gora: Grade, Art, and Time to Stop**

   Follow the railway through Miyanoshita and Kowakudani to Gora. Use the line's
   climb and switchbacks to make the terrain visible, then choose one museum
   with enough time to look. The Open-Air Museum is treated as a substantial
   stop, not one item in an exhausting museum checklist. A quick cafe pause,
   a proper lunch, and brought food are kept as three different decisions.

4. **穿越大涌谷：先看火山，再看富士山**<br>
   **大涌谷を越える：富士山より先に火山を見る**<br>
   **Cross Owakudani: See the Volcano Before Fuji**

   Read the fumarole field from Sounzan to Togendai, distinguish geology from
   spectacle, and explain the black egg without turning its longevity legend
   into a health claim. Current volcanic guidance, gas warnings, trail access,
   and ropeway operation must be rechecked immediately before publication and
   again by the traveler.

5. **芦之湖：神社、公鱼、湖岸**<br>
   **芦ノ湖：神社・わかさぎ・湖畔**<br>
   **Lake Ashi: Shrine, Wakasagi, and Shore**

   Connect Togendai, Moto-Hakone, Hakone Shrine, Hakone-machi, and the lake
   crossings without pretending that every boat or Fuji view is essential.
   Separate shrine tradition from established historical evidence, connect
   wakasagi to the lake rather than a generic food list, and give clear
   wet-weather and low-visibility choices.

6. **旧东海道与箱根关所：走一段路，读一种制度**<br>
   **旧東海道と箱根関所：道を歩き、制度を読む**<br>
   **Old Tokaido and the Checkpoint: Walk the Road, Read the System**

   Walk one manageable section of stone pavement, stop at Amasake Chaya, and
   read the reconstructed checkpoint as evidence of controlled movement on a
   difficult route. Terrain, surfaces, bus escape points, and daylight matter
   more than completing an arbitrary distance.

7. **温泉与旅馆的一夜：洗浴、晚餐、安静**<br>
   **温泉と旅館の一夜：入浴、夕食、静けさ**<br>
   **One Night in an Onsen Ryokan: Bathing, Dinner, and Quiet**

   Trace the change from the Edo-period seven springs and therapeutic stays to
   a wider resort landscape. Explain bathing practice, tattoo policies,
   private-bath choices, meal times, shoes, bedding, and checkout rhythms in a
   calm sequence. Do not imply that all properties share one rule or one kind
   of water.

8. **沿途怎么吃：黑鸡蛋、甘酒与晚餐时钟**<br>
   **道中で何を食べるか：黒たまご、甘酒、夕食の時計**<br>
   **Eat Along the Route: Black Eggs, Amazake, and the Dinner Clock**

   Organize food by place and time: a black egg at Owakudani, amazake and mochi
   on the old road, a ryokan meal that may set the evening schedule, and
   Odawara kamaboko only as gateway context. Avoid claiming that generic soba,
   kaiseki, or cafe dishes are uniquely Hakone food.

9. **箱根住哪一段：按地区选旅馆**<br>
   **山のどこに泊まるか：エリアで宿を選ぶ**<br>
   **Where to Stay: Choose a District Before a Property**

   Compare Yumoto and Tonosawa, Miyanoshita and Kowakudani, Gora, Sengokuhara,
   and the Lake Ashi side by next-morning route, evening meal constraints,
   luggage handling, slopes, and transport resilience. Named hotels appear
   only after current verification; district logic remains the durable advice.

10. **一、二、三天：给天气留下余地**<br>
    **一日・二日・三日：天気の余白を残す**<br>
    **One, Two, or Three Days: Leave Room for Weather**

    Build realistic first-visit, onsen-led, art-led, and reduced-mobility
    plans. Include transfers, meals, check-in, luggage, and rest. Every plan
    has a fog, rain, wind, or transport-disruption fallback rather than an
    idealized chain of attractions.

11. **箱根之外：只选一站**<br>
    **箱根の先：一駅だけ選ぶ**<br>
    **Beyond Hakone: Choose One Stop**

    Choose one continuation according to the onward journey: Odawara for the
    castle and eastern gateway, Mishima for a westbound exit, or Gotemba for
    the Fuji side. This is a decision chapter, not permission to combine all
    three with a full Hakone day. End with a dated departure checklist.

## Editorial Rules

- Open with a place, movement, surface, sound, or decision. Do not open with a
  ranking, slogan, or claim that Hakone is "where tradition meets nature."
- Keep the route visible. A paragraph that does not help the traveler choose,
  move, look, eat, stay, or recheck belongs in the fact ledger or is removed.
- Treat Hakone as a living volcanic area. Safety notices and restrictions are
  practical information, not dramatic decoration.
- Make weather uncertainty useful. Explain what changes when the ropeway
  stops, the lake disappears into cloud, or rain makes stone paving slippery.
- State clearly when a narrative comes from shrine tradition, local legend,
  institutional history, geological research, or current operator guidance.
- Do not publish current prices, hours, last departures, closures, or pass
  inclusions without an official URL and a visible `checked_at` date.
- Recommend a district before a named hotel, a meal context before a ranked
  restaurant, and one museum before a list of museums.
- Attraction figures show the actual place at useful scale. Aya-chan and Lala
  Xia are traveler-scale guides; they never replace the destination.

## Language Contract

- **Chinese:** use natural travel prose and standard simplified Chinese. Add
  reviewed pinyin ruby where it helps a Chinese learner read aloud; do not
  cover every punctuation-adjacent particle with visual noise.
- **Japanese:** write the Japanese pass independently, using familiar local
  forms such as `箱根湯本`, `強羅`, `大涌谷`, and `芦ノ湖`. Add reviewed
  furigana, including place-name readings that cannot be guessed reliably.
- **English:** favor route logic, terrain, and concrete traveler judgment.
  Retain Japanese names when they identify signs, stations, foods, or customs.
- The three languages align by meaning and evidence, not sentence-by-sentence
  literalism. Each block must read naturally on its own.
