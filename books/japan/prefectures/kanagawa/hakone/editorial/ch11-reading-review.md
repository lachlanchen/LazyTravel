## Chinese Pass

The Chinese pass begins with the onward ticket and the traveller's actual side
of the mountain. It keeps `小田原`, `三岛`, and `御殿场` as alternatives,
then gives each branch one practical stop and the history needed to read it.
Phrases that treated all travel time as interchangeable were removed. The
departure passage now works backwards from the reserved train and removes the
local stop before cutting food, water, toilet, or boarding margin.

Reviewed readings include `小田原（xiǎotiányuán）`,
`三岛（sāndǎo）`, `御殿场（yùdiànchǎng）`,
`元箱根（yuánxiānggēn）`, `东海道（dōnghǎidào）`,
`源兵卫川（yuánbīngwèichuān）`, and
`新桥浅间神社（xīnqiáo qiǎnjiān shénshè）`.

## Japanese Pass

The Japanese pass was edited as route-led guide prose rather than a literal
rendering of the English. It distinguishes `東側下山`, `元箱根`, and the
`桃源台・仙石原` side, and uses `乗車時刻から逆算` for the final clock.
Shrine tradition, recorded route history, and surviving buildings remain
separate. The text never promises a Fuji view, a through bus, bag storage, or
a walk in the river.

Reviewed readings include `小田原（おだわら）`, `三島（みしま）`,
`御殿場（ごてんば）`, `源兵衛川（げんべえがわ）`,
`三嶋大社（みしまたいしゃ）`, `新橋浅間神社（にいはしせんげんじんじゃ）`,
and `乗車時刻（じょうしゃじこく）`.

## English Pass

The English pass uses one decision line throughout: fix the onward train,
identify the exit already reached, deal with the bag, then add at most one
stop. Generic extension language and awkward clock metaphors were removed.
Odawara Castle is identified as a present reconstruction; Mishima Taisha's
unknown foundation is kept apart from later records and the 1866 buildings;
Gotemba remains useful even when Fuji is hidden.
