# Chapter 3 Fact Ledger: The Terracotta Army as Archaeology

Ledger date: `2026-08-14`. Destination gate: `china/cities/xian`.

This ledger controls the claims used in Chapter 3. *Xi'an Shihua* and the
family guide supply the older narrative sequence and questions to investigate;
neither controls present interpretation, visitor information, or excavation
counts. Museum records, UNESCO documentation, and archaeological research are
used to check those leads. The Chinese and English open guides are retained as
dated orientation references only; their fares, bus routes, hours, rankings,
and prose are not reused.

## Rejected Or Deferred Claims

| Claim | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| "The Terracotta Army is the world's eighth wonder." | Rejected as promotional language, even when repeated by institutional pages. It does not help the reader inspect archaeological evidence. |
| "There are exactly 8,000 warriors." | Rejected. Published figures mix excavated objects, estimates, horses, and different scopes. Use no whole-army total in running prose. |
| "Every face is unique" or "every figure depicts a real soldier." | Rejected. Visible variation does not prove individual portraiture. Production research supports organized local workshops and controlled variation. |
| "The pits preserve the Qin army exactly as it stood in battle." | Rejected. They are funerary constructions interpreted through archaeological context. |
| "Pit 3 was certainly headquarters" and named rooms had known military functions. | Rejected without qualification. Command and ritual readings are interpretations. |
| "Ancient chrome plating kept the bronze weapons from rusting." | Rejected. The chapter does not repeat the obsolete anti-rust-technology story. |
| "The colours disappear in four minutes" or another universal countdown. | Rejected. Primary conservation research supports rapid and serious instability, not a single theatrical timer for every object. |
| The 2013 guide's ticket prices, tourist buses, hotel listings, toilets, guides, and five-to-seven-year excavation forecast. | Rejected as stale. Current operations come only from dated official sources. |
| Combining the Terracotta Army, Huaqing Palace, Lishan, and the mausoleum park into one short stop because they share the word `Lintong`. | Deferred to Chapter 7. The visit burden and thematic coherence need a full nearby-area comparison. |

## Language Pass Briefs

- **Chinese:** write `秦始皇帝陵博物院`, `兵马俑坑`, and `丽山园` precisely.
  Prefer concrete verbs such as `清理`, `拼合`, `加固`, and `监测`; avoid
  slogan chains and reverential superlatives.
- **Japanese:** distinguish `兵馬俑坑`, `陵墓`, and `副葬坑`. Use
  `〜と解釈されている` where the function is inferential; do not translate
  Chinese promotional cadence.
- **English:** distinguish the mausoleum complex, burial mound, accessory pits,
  and museum halls. Prefer "funerary representation" to "frozen army" and
  "commonly interpreted" to certainty.

  the burial mound and the eastern warrior-pit sector at landscape scale, plus
