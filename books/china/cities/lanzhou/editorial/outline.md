# Lanzhou Editorial Outline

Title: **Lanzhou Pocket Travel Guide**.

Lanzhou is easiest to use when the traveler first understands its long,
narrow river valley. The city extends mainly east and west; the Yellow River,
the two sides of the valley, and a small number of crossings are more useful
for orientation than a flat list of districts. The book's main line is
therefore practical: **read the valley, arrive at the right gate, cross the
river deliberately, eat according to the city's daily rhythm, and leave from
the right gate for the next journey**.

This is a city travel guide, not a compressed history of Gansu and not a
catalogue of Yellow River slogans. History appears where it explains a place
the traveler can inspect: an old crossing, the iron bridge, the provincial
capital, the railway, a museum object, or a working waterwheel tradition. Food
appears by meal and setting, not as an unranked list. The city chapters remain
useful on their own; the final two chapters connect Lanzhou to a wider Gansu
trip without pretending that every regional destination is a nearby stop.

The book has exactly 11 chapters. The count is locked because each chapter
answers a different travel decision. Combining them would hide route choices;
splitting them further would create short chapters with repeated context.

Milestone status on `2026-08-22`: Chapters 1-8 are accepted in the B6 pocket
and synchronized website. Chapter 9 is the only active production gate.

## Chapter Spine

1. **先读河谷：黄河、两山与东西方向**<br>
   **まず河谷を読む：黄河、二つの山、東西の方向**<br>
   **Read the Valley First: River, Hills, and East-West**

   Establish the west-east river, the close valley sides, the elongated built
   area, and the relative positions of Qilihe, Xiguan, the central river
   crossing, Dongfanghong Square, Lanzhou Station, and the airport corridor.
   The opening map teaches one durable habit: decide whether the next stop is
   east, west, north bank, or south bank before choosing a vehicle. It also
   distinguishes schematic route distance from walking distance.

2. **到哪一站下：机场、西站与兰州站**<br>
   **どこで降りるか：空港・蘭州西駅・蘭州駅**<br>
   **Arrive at the Right Gate: Airport, West Station, or Lanzhou Station**

   Compare the current T3 airport transfer, Lanzhou West Railway Station,
   Lanzhou Railway Station, and the two-line metro network by final
   destination, luggage, arrival time, and next-morning plan. A train named
   for Lanzhou is not assumed to use the same station. Timetables, last
   services, fares, terminal assignments, and airport-bus routes remain dated
   facts and are checked close to travel.

3. **渡口怎样成为省城**<br>
   **渡し場はいかに省都になったか**<br>
   **How a Crossing Became a Provincial Capital**

   Follow one connected question rather than a dynasty parade: why did a
   difficult river crossing acquire walls, administration, bridges, railways,
   and industry? Distinguish Han-period Jincheng administration from the
   footprint of the present city, explain Lanzhou's Qing-period provincial
   rise, and use the 1909 iron bridge and later railway development as visible
   changes in movement. Omit a date or detail when independent sources do not
   agree.

4. **过一座桥，看两岸：黄河铁桥、白塔山与黄河边**<br>
   **橋を一つ渡り、両岸を見る：黄河鉄橋・白塔山と黄河沿い**<br>
   **Cross One Bridge, Read Both Banks: Yellow River Iron Bridge and White Pagoda Hill**

   Build one realistic central walk from the south bank across the Yellow River
   Iron Bridge (Zhongshan Bridge) to White Pagoda Hill Park, then choose one
   riverfront segment. The bridge is the route decision; the park is the
   north-bank viewpoint, not a disconnected extra stop. Explain what the
   floating-bridge and iron-bridge history changes in the view. Waterwheel sites
   and longer promenades are options, not compulsory extensions. River
   conditions, boat operation, and temporary access restrictions require
   same-day checks.

5. **在甘肃省博物馆读一条路**<br>
   **甘粛省博物館で一本の道を読む**<br>
   **Read One Route Through Gansu Provincial Museum**

   Treat the provincial museum as a substantial visit. Choose a coherent
   object route through prehistoric settlement, movement along the Hexi
   Corridor, and Buddhist-art connections instead of trying to summarize every
   gallery. Explain why objects found far from Lanzhou belong in a Lanzhou
   itinerary: the city is the point from which many travelers begin to read
   the province. Exhibitions, reservations, opening days, and object display
   status remain current information.

6. **按兰州的时钟吃：清晨一碗面，午后几样小吃**<br>
   **蘭州の時計で食べる：朝の一杯、午後の小吃**<br>
   **Eat by Lanzhou's Clock: A Morning Bowl and Afternoon Snacks**

   Begin with the practical sequence of ordering Lanzhou beef noodles in the
   morning: bowl, noodle width, additions, payment, and the pace of a working
   shop. Continue with niangpizi, huidouzi, tianpeizi, Lanzhou lily, and
   sanpaotai only where meal time and setting make sense. Separate protected
   craft descriptions from origin legends, never assume every shop has the
   same halal practice, and avoid health claims or restaurant rankings.

7. **从哪一处看全城：白塔山、兰山或五泉山**<br>
   **街をどこから見るか：白塔山・蘭山・五泉山**<br>
   **Choose One Height: White Pagoda Hill, Lanshan, or Wuquan Mountain**

   Compare three different reasons to climb: White Pagoda Hill for the central
   crossing, Lanshan for a broad valley view, and Wuquan Mountain for a city
   park and temple landscape. Choose one according to weather, daylight,
   mobility, and the rest of the route. Do not promise a clear skyline or turn
   all three into one day. Current cableway, vehicle, gate, and path conditions
   are checked independently.

8. **住在路线哪一段：先选地区，再选酒店**<br>
   **どの区間に泊まるか：ホテルより先にエリアを選ぶ**<br>
   **Where to Stay: Choose the Route Segment Before the Hotel**

   Compare Xiguan and the central river crossing, Qilihe and Lanzhou West,
   Dongfanghong Square and the university/station side, and an airport-area
   night by arrival gate, dinner, morning route, luggage, noise, and late
   return. Named properties appear only after current verification. A booking
   filter or generated room scene is never evidence for accessibility,
   cleanliness, view, or operating status.

9. **一、二、三天：给河谷留出空白**<br>
   **一日・二日・三日：河谷に余白を残す**<br>
   **One, Two, or Three Days: Leave Room in the Valley**

   Assemble realistic plans around one anchor and one crossing at a time. A
   short stay combines the museum or river core with a properly timed meal; a
   longer stay adds one height and slower neighborhood time. Each plan includes
   luggage, meals, museum closure, heat, cold, rain, poor visibility, and a
   usable exit instead of replacing every cancelled stop with another stop.

10. **离开兰州：把甘肃下一程接好**<br>
    **蘭州を出る：甘粛の次の行程につなぐ**<br>
    **Leaving Lanzhou: Connect the Next Gansu Leg**

    Decide whether the next long leg begins at Lanzhou West, Lanzhou Station,
    or the airport, and whether Lanzhou needs an arrival night, a full city
    day, or a departure buffer. Westbound rail, an eastbound return, and a
    flight are planning branches rather than promises of a particular service.
    The chapter dates every schedule-dependent statement and removes a local
    stop before risking a reserved onward journey.

11. **兰州周边：只选一个近郊日**<br>
    **蘭州周辺：郊外の一日を一つだけ選ぶ**<br>
    **Around Lanzhou: Choose One Nearby Day**

    Compare one culture-and-landscape day at Bingling Temple and Liujiaxia,
    where the Yellow River-Tao River confluence viewing platform is considered
    only when its road transfer fits the same-day route; one active landscape
    day at Xinglong Mountain; and a shorter geology-led option at Lanzhou Ink
    Danxia after current access has been verified. They are alternatives, not
    a chain. Boat operation, viewing-platform shuttles, weather closures, road
    transfers, reservations, and return margins are checked on official
    channels immediately before travel. Places that need an overnight stay
    belong to a later Gansu route, not this chapter.

## Editorial Rules

- Open with a route, place, object, meal, or decision. Do not open with a
  ranking, tourism slogan, or generic statement about old and new cultures.
- Keep the city visible. A paragraph must help the reader choose, move, look,
  eat, stay, or recheck; otherwise it belongs in the ledger or is removed.
- Use the Yellow River as orientation and evidence, not as a metaphor repeated
  on every page.
- Connect eras only through a named place or a documented change in movement.
  Never imply that one unchanged city persisted from Qin or Han times to the
  present.
- Distinguish Lanzhou, the wider municipal area, and Gansu Province. A museum
  object or regional food does not become a Lanzhou invention merely because
  it is displayed or served in the city.
- State clearly when a claim comes from an institutional history, protected
  heritage record, local tradition, memoir, open guide, or current operator.
- Do not publish current prices, hours, last departures, closures, reservation
  rules, hotel operations, or ticket inclusions without an official URL and a
  visible `checked_at` date.
- Recommend a district before a hotel, a meal context before a restaurant, one
  hill before three viewpoints, and one nearby day before a regional checklist.
- Attraction figures show the real place at useful scale. The four guides
  establish traveler scale but never replace the destination.

## Language Contract

- **Chinese:** use direct, natural simplified Chinese travel prose. Add reviewed
  pinyin ruby to Chinese text without burying punctuation or short function
  words in visual noise.
- **Japanese:** write a separate Japanese editorial pass, using stable local
  forms such as `蘭州`, `黄河`, `中山橋`, `白塔山`, and `甘粛省博物館`.
  Add reviewed furigana, especially for Chinese place and food names whose
  Japanese readings are not obvious.
- **English:** keep route logic and traveler judgment concrete. Preserve useful
  Chinese names in romanization where they identify signs, stations, foods, or
  booking fields.
- The three languages align by decision and evidence, not by literal sentence
  shape. Each language must read as edited prose on its own.

  hash-pinned four-guide references. Every non-map figure contains exactly
  Aya-chan, Lala Xia, Sasa-kun, and the Zhuangzi robot; no anonymous fifth
  traveler or historical eyewitness scene is permitted.
- Location-led figures must make Zhongshan Bridge, the river valley, the
  museum setting, a real food context, or the selected nearby destination
  recognizable at B6 size. The cast remains secondary.
- Reader-facing captions explain the place and what to notice. Generation
  method, hashes, factual limits, rejected variants, and visual QA stay in
  technical provenance.
- Only a verified B6 pocket PDF is distributed and hash-synced to Nutstore.
