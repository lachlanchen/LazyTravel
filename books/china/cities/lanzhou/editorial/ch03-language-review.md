# Chapter 3 Language Review

Status: Chinese, Japanese, and English accepted on `2026-08-21` after
independent line edits and compiled B6/mobile review.

## Shared Thread

All nine blocks follow one current walk. The temple establishes a visible
later old-city layer, the map leads west along Zhangye Road and north to the
bridge, and the history explains only the changes that altered this route.
Early Jincheng geography, the Wuquan/Chengguan core, Qing institutions, the
permanent bridge, and railway-era expansion remain distinct.

## Chinese

The Chinese pass uses direct place and movement verbs: `走进`, `向西走`,
`转向河边`, and `到桥南端`. It corrects the historical county name to `五泉`
and removes abstract expressions such as “季节性不确定” and “空间现实.” The
Qing paragraph keeps the four institutional dates but immediately tells the
reader that their order matters more than memorising them. The final callout
is `桥边只留四层`, not a theatrical instruction to stop a history lecture.

## Japanese

The Japanese pass reads as a separate guide rather than a sentence-by-sentence
copy. Chinese place and office names remain identifiable, while the prose uses
ordinary travel language such as `西へ進む`, `川へ向かう`, `入口で確認する`,
and `予定を組む`. `五泉` is distinguished from `金城`, and the bridge passage
replaces the translated phrase “季節的な不確実さ” with the concrete change
from a repeatedly maintained floating facility to a fixed crossing.

## English

The English pass keeps one practical subject per paragraph: enter the temple,
follow the axis, separate disputed geography, read the Qing sequence, stop at
the bridge, and divide the later city into valley segments. “Centre of
gravity,” “seasonal uncertainty,” and the one-brick millennium image were
removed. Administrative titles appear only where the chronology depends on
them, followed by a plain explanation of what accumulated in Lanzhou.

## Alignment Result

The three languages align by place, evidence, and travel decision, not by
sentence count. None claims a continuous Han-to-modern city, a surviving gate,
permanent temple access, the disappearance of every ferry, or exact execution
of the 1954 plan. Chapter 4 remains responsible for the detailed bridge and
White Pagoda Hill route.

Ruby, headings, callouts, captions, and source markers remain legible in the
45-page B6 milestone and the `390 px` site. No language required truncation or
literal reshaping to fit the layout.
