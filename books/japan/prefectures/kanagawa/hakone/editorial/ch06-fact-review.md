## Chapter Question

The chapter answers one question: **how much of the old Tokaido should a
traveler walk, and where should the walk end?** The usable sequence is Hakone
Checkpoint, the Moto-Hakone cedar avenue, one coherent stretch of stone paving,
and Amasake Chaya. History explains what is visible at each stop. It does not
interrupt the route with a detached Edo-period survey.

## Rejected Shortcuts

- "Every visible stone is untouched Edo-period material." Rejected. The site
  combines survival with later conservation and maintenance.
- "The road detour on Prefectural Road 732 is the replacement hike." Rejected.
  It is an official traffic detour, not a recommended leisure walk.
- "Incoming guns and outgoing women was the identical inspection rule at every
  checkpoint." Rejected. Hakone's own interpretation is more specific.
- "The checkpoint buildings have stood intact since around 1619." Rejected.
  The location is historical; the present buildings are evidence-based
  reconstructions completed in 2007.
- "Amasake Chaya should be presented as an Edo-period scene." Rejected. It is a
  working contemporary tea house with a traceable route and food history.
- "Route K guarantees the next useful bus." Rejected. Direction, destination,
  timetable, disruption, and the traveler's lodging must be checked live.
- Generated figures as exact documentary photographs. Rejected. They support
  place recognition, route scale, architecture, and food form without proving
  one exact moment or historical event.
