## Chapter Question

The chapter answers one question: **which single place can carry the journey
beyond Hakone without putting the onward reservation at risk?** The answer
comes from the traveller's actual side of the mountain and next rail journey:
Odawara for an eastern exit and castle stop, Mishima for a western Shinkansen
handoff, or Gotemba for the Fuji-side corridor. These are alternatives, not a
three-stop itinerary.

## Rejected Shortcuts

- "Do Odawara, Mishima, and Gotemba after Hakone." Rejected. The three places
  lie on different exit logics; combining them defeats the chapter.
- "Odawara Castle is a surviving Hojo keep." Rejected. The current keep is a
  twentieth-century reconstruction, while the Hojo defence extended around
  castle and town.
- "Every Shinkansen stops at Odawara or Mishima." Rejected. The traveller must
  check the booked train's stopping pattern.
- "The bus to Mishima or Gotemba is a guaranteed through connection." Rejected.
  Timetables, queues, road conditions, route patterns, and service states change.
- "Mishima Taisha was founded in a precise ancient year." Rejected. The shrine
  itself says that the founding date is unknown.
- A five-kilometre Mishima model course between a mountain bus and a reserved
  train. Rejected. The chapter offers one compact stop, with Genbe River as a
  separate fair-weather alternative.
- "Fuji will be visible from Gotemba." Rejected. The route remains useful in
  cloud; the figure cannot serve as a visibility forecast.
- Gotemba Premium Outlets as the default continuation. Rejected. Shopping is a
  separate, time-consuming purpose and not a substitute for the Fuji-side route.
- A universal luggage-storage promise at any station. Rejected. Availability,
  dimensions, hours, and acceptance are date-specific.

## Block Spine

1. Read a three-branch map: next ticket, current mountain exit, one stop, onward rail.
2. Use Odawara when the route already descends east; protect the train before walking to the castle.
3. Read the present keep as a reconstruction and the Hojo castle as a town-scale defence.
4. Use Mishima when leaving the south shore for a westbound mainline journey.
5. Choose Mishima Taisha for route history, or Genbe River for a short water walk, never both under a tight connection.
6. Keep shrine history, Yoritomo tradition, the Tokaido, and the present nineteenth-century buildings in their proper layers.
7. Use Gotemba only when the northern Hakone exit and the next journey make the Fuji-side branch useful.
8. Make Niihashi Sengen a small station-side pause; do not turn cloud, a spring, or a shrine into a promise.
9. Put luggage, food, local walking, the bus, and the onward train on one departure clock.
10. Close with a dated five-part check and remove the local stop before risking the reservation.
