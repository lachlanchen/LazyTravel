## Chapter Question

The chapter answers one question: **how much Hakone can the traveller finish
without sacrificing the booked night or the journey out?** One, two, and three
days are not shorter and longer checklists. Each has one principal place per
day, one meal or lodging commitment that cannot be improvised, and one route
that can be removed when visibility, wind, rain, crowding, or a service break
changes the mountain.

## Rejected Shortcuts

- "Hakone can be completed in one day." Rejected. A day can complete one
  coherent route; the destination has no completion checklist.
- "The classic loop is always the best first visit." Rejected. Entry, exit,
  weather, lodging, mobility, crowding, and live operation decide the useful
  direction and whether a crossing is sensible at all.
- A minute-by-minute schedule assembled from average ride times. Rejected.
  Queues, transfers, meals, walking, toilets, and disruptions are not captured
  by adding vehicle times.
- "Rainy day means museums." Rejected as a complete rule. A museum can still be
  closed or hard to reach, and a warning or network break can make direct exit
  the correct plan.
- "Barrier-free museum" means barrier-free Hakone. Rejected. Station, vehicle,
  stop, pavement, entrance, toilet, lodging, and bath are separate checks.
- A wheelchair, older traveller, or low-stamina traveller as a decorative
  scene. Rejected. The figure shows an ordinary arrival and a real route choice;
  the text supplies the facility-specific questions.
- Two art museums plus the full upper route in one day. Rejected. One major
  museum is an anchor, not a short stop between transfers.
- Moving hotels every night as proof of seeing more. Rejected. A second base is
  justified only when it removes more travel than the luggage move creates.
- Day-use onsen access without direct confirmation. Rejected. Bath admission,
  hours, tattoo rules, cleaning, towels, and access vary.

## Block Spine

1. Read a three-lane decision map: one anchor per day, one fixed commitment, one removable branch.
2. Use the clear one-day crossing only after an early start and live upper-route check.
3. Replace a broken upper chain with one coherent lower or indoor day; warnings can cancel sightseeing.
4. Build a one-night onsen plan around luggage, arrival, dinner, bath, breakfast, and checkout.
5. Give an art-led trip one major museum per day and move the outdoor-heavy visit to the better window.
6. Use three days as movable envelopes, with only one south-shore, old-road, or highland branch on Day 3.
7. Build a reduced-transfer, lower-strain day from one verified facility, seated rest, toilets, and a simple return.
8. Put luggage, lunch, check-in, dinner, rest, and the last useful connection on the same clock.
9. Check forecast and warnings, live transport, and booked commitments at three decision points.
10. Close with the cut order: optional purchase, second stop, fragile link; protect safety, rest, meals, and exit.
