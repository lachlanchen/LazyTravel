# Chapter 5 Fact Ledger: Inside the Wall

Ledger date: `2026-08-15`. Destination gate: `china/cities/xian`.

This chapter is a route through the present historic core, not a catalogue of
monuments. *Xi'an Shihua* supplies the contraction-and-expansion sequence that
explains the wall and the relocated Bell Tower. The family guide and the two
open guides identify the familiar visitor circuit, but their prices, opening
hours, population figures, business claims, and promotional descriptions are
not reused. *Living in Xian* is treated only as evidence of a popular modern
storytelling frame; its fictional street scenes support no factual claim.
Current municipal, heritage, and local-gazetteer records control the durable
history and expose points where official accounts disagree.

## Rejected Or Deferred Claims

| Claim | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Today's wall encloses Tang Chang'an, or the Bell Tower marks Tang Chang'an's centre. | Rejected. Chapter 2 established the different scales; Chapter 5 uses the Ming core. |
| The four main streets have survived unchanged from the Tang. | Rejected. Some alignments have earlier relationships, but the present street system is not an untouched Tang plan. |
| The wall must be cycled in full, with a fixed completion time. | Rejected. Rentals, opening points, weather, crowding, and ability vary. The route offers a short segment and a full-circuit decision. |
| Current tower performances, combination tickets, wall admission prices, bus routes, or opening hours from the 2013 guide or open-guide snapshots. | Rejected as stale. The chapter prints no such promise. |
| `回民街` is one street and all Hui neighborhood life occurs along Beiyuanmen. | Rejected. Use a network of lanes and institutions. |
| Present residents are simply descendants of Tang Arab or Persian merchants, or of a single named migrant group. | Rejected as an unsupported ancestry shortcut. |
| Every shop is Hui-owned, the district has a fixed tourist-guide population, or all local food is oily and spicy. | Rejected as unverifiable and reductive. |
| Huajue Lane Great Mosque was definitively founded in 742. | Rejected as settled fact because official gazetteers disagree. The conflict is stated once without turning the chapter into a dating dispute. |
| The mosque is a "paradox" because it has Chinese roofs rather than a dome. | Rejected. Architectural forms travel and adapt; the institution's religious use is not made less Islamic by local building traditions. |
| Photographing a prayer or a residential doorway is acceptable because the district is a tourist attraction. | Rejected. Public access does not remove ordinary consent and worship boundaries. |
| A catalogue of snacks and old restaurants. | Deferred to Chapter 6, where ingredients, ordering, and food context can be treated properly. |
| Detailed metro, bus, taxi, and airport instructions. | Deferred to Chapter 8 so volatile transport information has one controlled home. |

## Language Pass Briefs

- **Chinese:** distinguish `城墙`, `城内`, `钟楼`, `鼓楼`, `北院门`, `回坊`,
  `化觉巷清真大寺`, and `礼拜大殿`. Avoid `梦回长安`, `古今交融`,
  `烟火气`, and strings of promotional four-character phrases.
- **Japanese:** use `城壁内`, `鐘楼`, `鼓楼`, `回族街区`, and
  `化覚巷清真大寺` consistently. Explain the disputed foundation date in one
  direct sentence. Do not translate `回坊` as an exotic bazaar.
- **English:** prefer "inside the wall," "Hui neighborhood," "Great Mosque
  of Huajue Lane," and "prayer hall." Avoid "timeless maze," "sensory
  overload," "East meets West," "hidden gem," and "authentic locals."

## Visual Evidence Rules

- `asset-xian-inside-wall-route-map` uses two scales: the full Ming-wall
  rectangle and a detailed central walking area. The wall, named streets, and
  site positions come from a pinned OpenStreetMap extract; route lines are an
  original editorial plan and are not live navigation.
- The route map separates a core route from optional detours. No restaurant,
  current timetable, price, or bicycle service appears on the map.
- The chapter figure shows the physical transition from a busy lane to a
  tree-shaded courtyard threshold. It is an editorial scene, not a record of a
  particular doorway, worshipper, shop, or moment at Huajue Lane Great Mosque.
- Reader-facing captions describe the spatial cue and what to notice. Generator
  details, prompt, checksum, rights, and visual QA remain in project provenance
  rather than interrupting the chapter.
- The figure follows the restrained natural-light treatment of the previous
  chapters: plausible materials, ordinary contemporary clothing, no costume,
  legible signage, logo, watermark, dramatic lighting, or invented ritual.
