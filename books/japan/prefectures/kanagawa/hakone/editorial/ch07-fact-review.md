## Chapter Question

The chapter answers one question: **how should a traveler arrange one ryokan
night so that arrival, bathing, dinner, sleep, and departure work together?**
It follows the stay in order. History explains why onsen lodging became part of
Hakone travel, but the reader's reservation and the individual property's rules
control every practical decision. Chapter 9 will compare lodging districts;
this chapter does not rank hotels.

## Rejected Shortcuts

- "Hakone has seventeen identical kinds of water." Rejected. The count belongs
  to changing resort geography, and supplied water further weakens any simple
  address-to-source assumption.
- "An onsen cures the aches of a travel day." Rejected. The chapter explains
  bathing practice and history without medical promises.
- "Tattoos are forbidden in Hakone," or "tattoos are accepted in Hakone."
  Rejected. Current rules differ by property and sometimes by bath.
- "A private bath is automatically natural onsen water." Rejected. The room,
  bath, source, and reservation method must be checked separately.
- "A proper ryokan always serves kaiseki in the room and lays out a futon."
  Rejected. Plans, dining spaces, beds, rooms, and service styles vary.
- "Accepting floor sitting, public nudity, or a late meal proves respect for
  Japanese culture." Rejected. Privacy, diet, mobility, and sleep needs are
  legitimate booking criteria, not failures of authenticity.
- A generic multi-course dinner as uniquely Hakone food. Rejected. Chapter 8
  handles place-specific food; this chapter explains how a booked meal governs
  the evening.
- Generated bath, meal, or arrival scenes as documentary property photographs.
  Rejected. Figures illustrate the travel sequence without identifying or
  promising one real property's architecture, meal, or facilities.

## Block Spine

1. Stop sightseeing in time to arrive before the property's stated deadline.
2. Show the one-night sequence and the late-arrival branch without universal times.
3. Move from seven springs and therapeutic stays to seventeen spring districts, then explain why the booked bath still needs verification.
4. Prepare for a shared bath at the dry threshold, with privacy intact.
5. Give the wash, rinse, `kakeyu`, towel, hair, phone, photography, and quiet sequence.
6. Choose among public and private baths by policy, water, timing, privacy, and access.
7. Treat dinner as an appointment attached to the room plan, not a floating restaurant reservation.
8. Confirm meal location, ingredients, dietary requests, and the consequence of delay.
9. Choose futon, bed, seating, steps, shower, and nighttime movement for actual comfort.
10. Recheck morning-bath cleaning, breakfast, checkout, payment, luggage, and onward transport before sleep.
