## Shared Route

All ten blocks answer one practical question: how many full Lanzhou days remain
after luggage, a seated meal, reservations, weather, and the return are placed
on the clock? A one-day reader chooses the river or museum. Two days keep those
anchors separate. A third day adds one hill only when the second day did not
already include a full White Pagoda Hill climb; otherwise it becomes a slower
old-city day.

The chapter does not summarize earlier attractions as a checklist. It reuses
their accepted decisions to build a route: west for the museum, centre for the
bridge, one complete ascent and descent, then a dependable return. Severe rain
now leads to reliable shelter or rest, not an outdoor fallback.

## Chinese

The Chinese pass uses ordinary planning language such as `住宿晚数`, `放好行李`,
`整天互换`, `只留一顿坐下来的正餐`, and `返程已定`. It removed abstract forms
that treated a second hill as proof of a substantial trip. Arrival and checkout
days are described through usable time rather than hotel-night arithmetic.

The weather paragraph distinguishes three decisions: use the river when the
museum is unavailable and outdoor conditions are suitable; use the booked
museum when a hill is unsuitable; and stay in reliable shelter or rest during
heavy rain. The old-city walk appears only when city walking remains safe.

## Japanese

The Japanese pass was edited independently around natural itinerary choices:
`宿泊数だけで決めない`, `予約枠と開館日に合わせる`, `川沿いを一区間だけ歩く`,
and `丸一日使える日から組み立てる`. It removed literal phrases such as
`評判の一店のために`, `帰路を一部分だけ歩く`, and `完全な日`.

Museum, hill, and departure vocabulary now reads as ordinary travel Japanese.
Heavy rain uses `確かな屋内に移るか休む`; a ground-level old-city route is not
presented as safe merely because it avoids a slope.

## English

The English pass replaces abstract “height” and “usable exit” language with
`hill`, `departure`, and `reliable return`. It also removed translated phrases
such as `one morning bowl`, `recover something missed`, `findspot`, and
`arrival and departure margin`. The museum, bridge, meal, baggage, and cut
order can now be followed without interpreting editorial shorthand.

## Alignment Result

The three languages align by decision and consequence, not sentence shape.
None promises an opening, reservation, clear view, path, exact transfer, named
restaurant, or last service. Source claims remain dated, while the itinerary
rules stay durable. The prose is closed unless compiled-page or responsive
review exposes a concrete language or layout defect.
