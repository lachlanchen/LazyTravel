## Chapter Job

Chapter 3 answers one question while the reader walks through the present
centre: why did this crossing become a place where regional administration,
roads, bridges, railways, and industry accumulated?

The route begins around the Lanzhou Prefecture City God Temple on Zhangye Road,
uses the old east-west street and former gate positions to read the later city
core, and finishes at the south approach to Zhongshan Bridge. It does not turn
Lanzhou into an uninterrupted city stretching from the Han dynasty to today.
The detailed bridge-and-White-Pagoda-Hill visit belongs to Chapter 4.

## Factual Boundaries

- `金城郡`, `金城县`, `五泉县`, `兰州`, `兰州府`, and the present municipality
  are different administrative or geographic objects. Similar names do not
  prove an unchanged city footprint.
- The book states `81 BCE` for the establishment of Jincheng Commandery. It
  does not assign one certain modern street corner to its original seat.
- Local records and later scholarship disagree about the identification and
  movement of early Jincheng County. The map therefore shows an uncertainty
  band rather than an invented ancient-city polygon.
- The Sui/Tang/Song sequence is used only to explain why the later old-city
  core and river crossing became connected. It is not a complete dynasty
  survey.
- The Qing dates describe institutions moving or being reorganized. They do
  not mean that a modern provincial capital appeared fully formed in one year.
- The `1909` bridge completion is enough for this chapter. Dimensions,
  imported components, cost, payment terms, and renaming chronology remain out
  unless Chapter 4 reconciles them from stronger engineering evidence.
- The railway and 1954 plan explain later east-west expansion. A plan is not
  evidence that each proposed road, factory, or district was completed exactly
  as drawn.
- The current City God Temple remains a real civic and heritage place, but a
  dated event does not establish permanent opening hours, free entry, or access
  to every hall.

## Rejected Draft Claims

- “Lanzhou has stood here continuously for more than two thousand years.”
- “Jincheng means the city was literally made of gold” or any single unsourced
  etymology.
- “The modern Zhangye Road follows a Han street.”
- “Lanzhou became Gansu's capital in 1666” as a one-date summary.
- “The iron bridge ended the importance of ferries everywhere on the river.”
- “The 1954 plan created the city exactly as it appears today.”
- Any sentence that makes the guides witnesses to a historical event.

## Editorial Result Required

The chapter passes when a reader can walk the present core and explain, in
plain language, why four different layers matter. Dates stay subordinate to
visible places and changes in movement. The closing sentence must place the
reader at the bridge approach, ready for Chapter 4, without summarizing the
whole history again.
