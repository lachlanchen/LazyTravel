# Chapter 2 Language Review

Status: Chinese, Japanese, and English passes completed on `2026-08-21`.

## Shared Thread

Every block answers one arrival question: which exact gate is printed on the
booking, which part of Lanzhou comes first, and what fallback still works if a
connection fails. Airport T3, Lanzhou West, and Lanzhou Station remain three
distinct decisions. History and later sightseeing routes do not interrupt the
arrival sequence.

## Chinese

The Chinese pass keeps the official labels `中川机场T3`, `中川机场东`,
`兰州西站`, `兰州西站北广场`, `兰州站`, and `兰州火车站` distinct. Sentences
begin with the action a traveller takes: read the ticket, place the first stop,
check the live service, or keep a fallback. The final west-station and
Lanzhou-station passages were revised to remove translated constructions such
as “把换乘写出来” and to state the route choice directly.

## Japanese

The Japanese pass preserves Chinese sign forms where the traveller must match
what appears on a ticket or station sign, while the surrounding prose reads as
natural Japanese. `まず西側をまとめる` and
`西へ向かう乗り継ぎを先に決める` replace literal descriptions of crossing
the city or “showing” a transfer. Transport checks use ordinary verbs such as
`確認する`, `決める`, and `比較する` rather than administrative phrasing.

## English

The English pass uses short route decisions rather than mirroring Chinese word
order. “Plan the western stops first” and “plan the westbound transfer” state
the two station choices plainly. The airport section separates flight, rail,
coach, road transfer, lodging, and fallback without inventing a universal best
mode.

## Alignment Result

All nine blocks carry the same travel decision, evidence boundary, and asset
references in the three languages. The wording and sentence order differ where
natural. No version freezes a fare, timetable, train number, platform, exit,
pickup zone, or journey-time promise.
