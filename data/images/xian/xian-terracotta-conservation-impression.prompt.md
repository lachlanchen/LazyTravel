# Terracotta Conservation Work Impression

Use case: `photorealistic-natural`

Asset type: full-width editorial figure for the B6 LazyTravel pocket book and
its synchronized responsive website.

Primary request: create a photorealistic editorial image that gives a reader a
credible visual sense of archaeological conservation work associated with
terracotta figures. Show a quiet, modern conservation workspace where two
conservators carefully examine and align several large, clearly broken
terracotta sculpture fragments on a padded neutral worktable. The fragments
should suggest parts of a life-size armoured Qin-period funerary figure without
forming or copying a known museum object.

People and handling: two East Asian adult conservation professionals in plain
neutral work coats and nitrile gloves; natural, focused posture; faces small,
unposed, and not individually identifiable. Include restrained real tools such
as a soft brush, calipers, cotton swabs, archival trays, and one task light.
Hands, gloves, tools, and fragment joins must look physically plausible.

Style and composition: documentary editorial photography, landscape 3:2,
eye-level three-quarter view, soft neutral museum-laboratory light, honest clay
texture, muted earth and grey colour, generous negative space, one clear focal
plane. The scene should remain legible when printed about 105 mm wide in a B6
book.

Accuracy boundary: this is an interpretive conservation-work impression, not a
photograph of a real excavation, laboratory, conservator, or named artefact.
Keep the room generic and contemporary. Do not recreate a specific published
photograph or a complete famous warrior.

Constraints: no standing ranks, no tourist crowd, no dramatic discovery pose,
no intact tomb chamber, no fantasy palace, no cinematic dust, no sepia filter,
no exaggerated damage, no loose bones, no weapons, no ancient costumes, no
computer screens, no labels, no legible text, no logos, no watermark, no
decorative border.
